O-Ta-Ko Theory — Axiom 9:   The 1-2-1 Distribution Law

Axiom 9 — The 1-2-1 Distribution Law

The 1-2-1 Law is the distribution rule governing the internal structure of an O-Ta configuration.

In a basic O-Ta structure, the distribution follows the pattern:

1 → 2 → 1

For example, when comparing the values arranged along the vertical structure:

1 → 1
4 → 2
7 → 1

the central section contains the greatest concentration, while the two outer sections contain smaller corresponding portions.

The 1-2-1 Law describes the structural distribution, not merely the numerical value of an individual point.

Even when the O-Ta structure is stretched, compressed, or otherwise scaled, the 1-2-1 distribution remains structurally unchanged as long as the configuration itself remains an O-Ta structure.

Therefore, deformation may change the numerical values or scale of the configuration, but it does not by itself eliminate the 1-2-1 Law.

Axiom 11: Recursion

If the head shape is a new 10-Ta, then the entire set of rules is applied again.

Example:

Cell 5 contains the head shape.

The head shape is then divided into a 3×3 structure.

Therefore, the 1-2-1 Law is reapplied.

Head shape→3×3→reapply the 1-2-1 Law

Axiom 12: Deformation

If an O-Ta is only stretched or deformed, then the 1-2-1 Law does not change.

Only the values change.

Model Boundary

However, if the O-Ta becomes concave, cut, or otherwise structurally broken, it is no longer an O-Ta.

Stretching / deformation→1-2-1 Law unchanged
	​

Only the values change
	​

Concave / cut / structural break→outside the O-Ta model
	​


Model boundary: once the structure is concave, cut, or otherwise broken, it is no longer considered O-Ta.
